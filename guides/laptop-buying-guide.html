What to Check Before Buying a Laptop

A practical checklist for laptop processors, RAM, SSDs, display, battery and warranty.

1. Identify the exact processor

Do not judge by “Core i5” or “Core i7” alone. Check the full processor model and generation. Compare the exact chips for your workload.

2. Check RAM

RAM (Random Access Memory) helps keep active apps ready. 8 GB may be a basic starting point for general tasks; 16 GB can be more comfortable for multitasking. Confirm whether memory is soldered or upgradeable.

3. Check SSD capacity

An SSD usually makes startup and app loading faster than a traditional hard drive. A 128 GB drive can fill quickly. Check upgrade compatibility and capacity.

4. Inspect the screen and battery

Check resolution, brightness, dead pixels and physical damage. For used laptops, ask about battery health and test how quickly charge drops.

5. Test ports and core functions

Test charging, USB ports, audio, webcam, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, keyboard, touchpad and hinges. Look for overheating, unusual fan noise or liquid damage.

6. Check warranty and total cost

Confirm return terms, warranty status, seller reputation and operating-system activation. Include delivery, taxes, repairs and upgrades in the real price.

Quick checklist

  • Exact CPU model identified.
  • RAM capacity and upgrade limits confirmed.
  • SSD capacity and upgrade options checked.
  • Display, battery, keyboard, ports and Wi-Fi tested.
  • Warranty, return terms and seller reputation checked.
